# Assessing Progress

> **TL;DR** — Judge muscle progress **slowly and with multiple signals**, because any single measure is noisy. Real muscle gain is slow — roughly **1–2 lb/month for beginners, far less for advanced lifters** — so you assess it over **months**. Combine **performance trends** (are lifts progressing?), **bodyweight trend**, **tape measurements**, and **photos**, and remember that short-term changes are dominated by fat, water, and glycogen — not muscle.

**Quick recommendations**
- Expect **slow muscle gain**: ~0.25–0.5%/wk bodyweight for those still gaining; much less for advanced lifters. **[Grade B]**
- Read **direction** at about **4 weeks**; save the **verdict** on a block for **8–12+ weeks**, triangulating performance, weight trend, measurements, and photos. **[Grade B]**
- **Standardise every measurement** — fasted, rested, hydrated, same time of day. Unstandardised conditions move body-composition readings by more than a month of real change[^kerr-2017-measurement-error]. **[Grade B]**
- Separate **muscle from fat and water** — a scale jump overnight is not muscle. **[Grade B]**
- If **lifts are progressing** at a good bodyweight trend, you're on track even if the mirror is slow to show it. **[Grade B]**

## Practical Application

**Set realistic rates.** Muscle accrues slowly. Rough expectations (highly individual):

| Stage | Plausible muscle gain | Metric |
|-------|-----------------------|--------|
| Beginner (year 1) | ~1–2 lb/month | ~0.5–0.9 kg/month |
| Intermediate | ~0.5–1 lb/month | ~0.2–0.5 kg/month |
| Advanced | A few lb per *year* | ~1–2 kg/year |

Chasing faster than this by eating in a large surplus just adds fat (see [Energy Balance](../04-nutrition/energy-balance.md)).

**Triangulate.** No single number is trustworthy alone:
- **Performance** rising over weeks → the stimulus is working.
- **Bodyweight trend** (weekly average) in the expected range → you're fueling gain without excess.
- **Tape measurements** at consistent sites creeping up → real size change.
- **Photos** under identical conditions over months → the visual confirmation.

When these agree, you have your answer. When bodyweight jumps but measurements and photos don't, it's fat/water, not muscle.

**Two windows, not one.** These measures answer different questions at different speeds, which is why "how long before I know?" has two answers:

| Question | Window | What you're reading |
|---|---|---|
| Is this working at all? | **~4 weeks** | Direction — are lifts climbing and is bodyweight moving the way the goal wants? |
| Was this block a success? | **8–12+ weeks** | Magnitude — enough signal to separate real size change from noise |
| Should I change something now? | **~3–4 weeks flat** | A genuine stall, not a bad fortnight — see [logging and plateaus](logging-and-plateaus.md) |

[What to track](what-to-track.md) covers which numbers to record in the first place; this page is about how long to wait before believing them.

**Standardise, or you're measuring your breakfast.** The single highest-leverage thing you can do is take every measurement under the same conditions: **overnight fasted, rested, hydrated, same time of day**. Kerr and colleagues tested exactly this in athletes and found non-standardised presentation produced substantially large errors in fat-mass estimates across surface anthropometry and the multi-compartment models, with DXA and BOD POD holding up only when acute food and fluid intake stayed **under about 500 g**[^kerr-2017-measurement-error]. Half a litre of water is enough to move the reading — which is the whole reason a single measurement never decides anything here.

## The Evidence

**Muscle measurement is noisy and method-dependent**, so progress must be read over time and across signals. Haun and colleagues (2019) showed that hypertrophy estimates vary by measurement tool and are confounded by **swelling, edema, and fluid shifts**, especially in the short term[^haun-2019-measurement]. **[Grade B]** This is the evidence basis for long assessment windows and multi-signal triangulation rather than reacting to any single reading. Realistic rate expectations follow from the trainability curve (see [Foundations → Stimulus, Fatigue & Adaptation](../00-foundations/stimulus-fatigue-adaptation.md)), where gains decelerate with training age. **[Grade B]**

## Key Uncertainties & Nuance
- **Rate-of-gain figures are population averages** — genetics, age, sex, and diet shift them substantially (see [genetics and anthropometry](../06-individualization/genetics-and-anthropometry.md) and [training status](../06-individualization/training-status.md)).
- **The 4-week / 8–12-week split is a practical convention**, not a measured threshold — no trial has established the window at which a hypertrophy signal becomes reliable, so treat both numbers as sensible defaults rather than findings.
- **How much your weight swings day to day isn't quantified here.** It is dominated by food, water and glycogen rather than tissue, but this Bible doesn't yet carry a sourced figure for the normal range — which is exactly why the advice is to read a multi-week trend rather than to memorise a tolerance band.
- **Recomposition confounds the scale** — beginners/returnees can gain muscle while bodyweight barely moves, so lean on measurements/photos/performance.
- **Strength can outpace size early** (neural gains), so don't expect them to track perfectly.
- **Consistency of measurement conditions** is the difference between useful data and noise.
