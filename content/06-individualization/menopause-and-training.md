# Menopause & Training

> **TL;DR** — Menopause (the fall in estrogen) speeds up the natural loss of muscle and, especially, **bone** — but resistance training is the single most effective countermeasure, and the training itself barely changes. The same **hard, progressive lifting** that builds muscle at any age also **defends bone and lean mass** through and after the transition. Push protein a little higher, prioritise **loading the skeleton** with challenging compound lifts, and expect recovery and sleep to feel different — but the fundamentals (volume, effort, progression) are unchanged. Some women use **hormone therapy (HRT/MHT)**; that's a medical decision to make with a doctor, not a training variable.

**Quick recommendations**
- **Keep lifting hard and progressively.** Resistance training preserves and builds muscle and bone through and after menopause — it's protective, not optional. **[Grade B]**
- **Load the skeleton.** Prioritise challenging compound, weight-bearing lifts (squats, hinges, presses, rows) at moderate-to-higher intensity — bone adapts to meaningful mechanical load. **[Grade B]**
- **Protein a touch higher** (~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day). Ageing plus estrogen loss blunt the muscle-building response, so don't skimp. **[Grade B]** (see [Protein](../04-nutrition/protein.md))
- **HRT is a medical decision.** Discuss it with your doctor — it can influence muscle and bone, but it's not a training lever. **[Grade D]**
- **Adjust to recovery, don't stop.** Sleep and recovery often shift; tune volume to what you recover from rather than backing off training. **[Grade D]**

## Practical Application

**The training barely changes — that's the reassuring part.** Menopause doesn't rewrite the rules of building or keeping muscle. The variables that mattered before (enough hard [volume](../01-training-variables/volume.md), real [effort](../01-training-variables/proximity-to-failure.md), [progressive overload](../03-programming/periodization-and-progression.md)) still matter, in the same way. What changes is the *context*: the hormonal tailwind that used to make gains and recovery a little easier is gone, so consistency and adequate protein carry more of the load.

**Bone is the headline.** Estrogen protects bone, so its decline accelerates bone loss — the real risk of the menopausal transition. Resistance training (and impact/weight-bearing work) is one of the few things that meaningfully defends bone density, and it does so best at **challenging loads**. Anchor the week in loaded compound lifts you progress over time; this is the same training that builds muscle, so you get both from one investment.

**Protein and recovery.** With age-and-menopause "anabolic resistance", the muscle response to a given dose of protein and training is a little blunted — the answer is not to train less but to make each stimulus count: keep protein toward the higher end, keep sets close to failure, and protect [sleep](../05-recovery/sleep.md). If recovery feels harder, adjust volume to what you can recover from rather than stopping.

**On HRT.** Many women consider menopausal hormone therapy, and it can affect muscle and bone. Whether it's right for you is a medical decision to make with your doctor, weighing your own history — it sits outside training and this guide doesn't advise for or against it.

## The Evidence

**Resistance training defends bone in postmenopausal women.** Zhao and colleagues' 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis found resistance training significantly improves bone mineral density at the **lumbar spine and femoral neck** in postmenopausal women, and identified **moderate-to-higher intensity performed about three times a week** as the most effective dose[^zhao-2025-postmenopausal-bmd]. **[Grade B]** That is the same "train hard and often enough" prescription the rest of this knowledge base gives for muscle — bone and muscle are defended by the same work.

**And it preserves muscle and function with age.** Radaelli and colleagues' 2025 network meta-analysis in older adults found resistance-training **volume drives gains in lean mass, lower-body hypertrophy, strength, and physical function**, with a dose-response like that seen in younger trainees[^radaelli-2025-older-adults-volume]. **[Grade B]** Menopause overlaps this ageing window, so the message holds: doing enough hard sets is what keeps muscle on the frame.

**Protein supports the response.** Meta-analytic evidence shows protein supplementation augments resistance-training gains in muscle mass and strength, with the benefit coming from **meeting a sufficiently high total daily intake**[^morton-2018-protein-meta]. **[Grade A]** Given the blunted response with age and estrogen loss, aiming toward the higher end (~1.6–2.2 g/kg) is a low-risk way to protect that stimulus.

## Key Uncertainties & Nuance
- **The exact optimal training dose is still debated** — the meta-analytic signal is clear (train, at meaningful load, a few times a week) but the finer parameters (sets, precise intensity) vary across studies.
- **HRT's independent effect on muscle and bone, beyond training, is a genuinely medical and evolving question** — deliberately out of scope here; it belongs with your doctor.
- **Most trials are in previously-untrained women**, so effect sizes reflect starting from a low base; an already-trained woman defends more than she dramatically builds — which is exactly the point (defence is the goal).
- **Perimenopause vs postmenopause** differ (fluctuating vs low estrogen), and symptoms (sleep, joint aches, energy) vary widely — adjust volume and expectations to how you actually feel and recover, without stopping.
