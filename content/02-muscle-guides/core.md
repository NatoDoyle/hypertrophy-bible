# Core (Abdominals & Obliques)

> **TL;DR** — The core is three jobs: the **rectus abdominis** (the "six-pack" — it flexes the spine), the **obliques** (they rotate, side-bend, and *resist* rotation), and the **deep core** (the transverse abdominis and friends that brace the trunk). Grow the visible abs by **loading spinal flexion** and progressing it — weighted/cable crunches and hanging raises — not by grinding out 100 bodyweight reps. Add a little **anti-rotation/rotation** for the obliques. The deep core is mostly trained for free by your heavy compounds. And the big caveat: **visible abs are a body-fat outcome** (a [nutrition](../04-nutrition/index.md) job), not a crunch-volume one — you can't crunch off belly fat.

**Quick recommendations**
- Train abs with **added resistance** and progress it, like any muscle — not endless unloaded reps. **[Grade C]**
- Cover **loaded spinal flexion** (crunch-type) plus some **anti-rotation/rotation** for the obliques. **[Grade C]**
- ~**6–16 hard sets/week** is plenty; you don't need daily high-rep ab work. **[Grade C]**
- **Abs showing = low body fat**, achieved through diet — direct ab work builds the muscle, it doesn't burn the fat over it. **[Grade B]**

## Practical Application

Treat the core as regions with different tools. Picks are ordered for **hypertrophy** — favouring loadable, progressible movements over unloaded endurance work — the same way the app's engine ranks them.

**Region 1 — Rectus abdominis (the "six-pack").** *Job: spinal flexion — crunching the ribcage toward the pelvis.*
1. [Cable crunch](../../data/exercises/cable-crunch.json) — the backbone: freely loadable and progressible through a full range, so you can actually overload the abs.
2. [Machine crunch](../../data/exercises/machine-crunch.json) — the same loadable flexion with fixed, stable resistance.
3. [Weighted crunch](../../data/exercises/weighted-crunch.json) — add a plate/dumbbell once bodyweight crunches are easy.
4. [Hanging leg raise](../../data/exercises/hanging-leg-raise.json) — a harder movement that emphasises the lower rectus; add ankle weights to keep progressing.
5. [Ab-wheel rollout](../../data/exercises/ab-wheel-rollout.json) — loads the abs in a lengthened, anti-extension position; advanced, so build up to it.

*Cue:* think "spine flexes" — round the back to bring ribs toward hips, don't just hip-hinge; a controlled squeeze under load beats speed.

**Region 2 — Obliques (rotation, anti-rotation, side-bend).** *Job: twist the trunk, and — just as much — resist twisting.*
1. [Cable woodchop](../../data/exercises/cable-woodchop.json) — loaded rotation through a full arc.
2. [Pallof press](../../data/exercises/pallof-press.json) / [band Pallof press](../../data/exercises/band-pallof-press.json) — anti-rotation: you brace against a sideways pull.
3. [Kettlebell side bend](../../data/exercises/kettlebell-side-bend.json) — loaded lateral flexion under a stretch.
4. [Side plank](../../data/exercises/side-plank.json) — an anti-side-bend isometric for stability.

*Cue:* move (or resist) from the trunk, not the arms; keep the hips square on anti-rotation work.

**Region 3 — Deep core / bracing (transverse abdominis).** *Job: stiffen the trunk so force transfers.* Mostly trained **implicitly**: heavy [squats](../../data/exercises/barbell-back-squat.json), [deadlifts](../../data/exercises/conventional-deadlift.json), standing presses, and loaded [carries](../../data/exercises/kettlebell-suitcase-carry.json) all demand hard bracing. Add a [plank](../../data/exercises/plank.json), [dead bug](../../data/exercises/dead-bug.json), or suitcase carry only if you want dedicated stability work — these build *bracing*, not six-pack *size*.

**Putting a week together.** For most people: **2–4 loaded rectus sets** (a cable/machine crunch or hanging raise) plus **2–4 oblique sets** (a rotation or anti-rotation move), a couple of times a week, progressed like any lift. Your compounds cover the deep-core bracing. That's it — abs don't need special daily treatment.

## The Evidence

There is little ab-*specific* hypertrophy research, so the guidance is the **general hypertrophy principles applied to the abs**: growth requires sufficient **mechanical tension and volume with progression**[^schoenfeld-2017-volume-dose-response][^schoenfeld-2010-mechanisms], which for abs means added resistance rather than high-rep bodyweight work. **[Grade C]** Whether abs are *visible*, though, is a body-fat question, not a training-volume one — and **spot reduction is a myth**: Vispute and colleagues had participants perform six weeks of abdominal exercise and found it **did not reduce abdominal subcutaneous fat or overall body composition** (it improved muscular endurance, but not fatness)[^vispute-2011-ab-exercise-fat]. **[Grade B]** So direct ab work builds the muscle; **diet reveals it**.

## Key Uncertainties & Nuance
- **Ab genetics** (the number and symmetry of the tendinous intersections that create the "six-pack" blocks) are fixed; training changes size, not the pattern.
- **Anti-movement core training** (planks, Pallof presses) is valuable for function and bracing but is not the most effective way to *grow* the rectus abdominis — loaded flexion is.
- **"Lower abs" vs "upper abs"** is largely one sheet of muscle; hanging raises bias the lower portion a little, but you can't isolate a region the way the marketing implies.
