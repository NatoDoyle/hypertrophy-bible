# Back (Lats, Upper Back, Rear Delts & Spinal Erectors)

> **TL;DR** — "Back" is really **four jobs in one region**: the **lats** (the wing-like muscles that pull the arms down and back — they create width), the **upper back** (mid/lower traps and rhomboids that squeeze the shoulder blades together — that's thickness), the **rear delts** (small, easy to under-train), and the **spinal erectors** (extend and brace the spine, mostly loaded indirectly). Train width with a **vertical pull** and thickness with a **horizontal pull (row)**, emphasise a **full stretch** at the start of every rep, throw the rear delts a few dedicated sets, and let the erectors get most of their work from your heavy compounds. The back tolerates **high weekly volume** — use it.

**Quick recommendations**
- Every week, do **at least one vertical pull** (pulldown/pull-up, for width) **and one horizontal pull** (row, for thickness). **[Grade C]**
- **Emphasise the stretch**: full overhead reach on pulldowns, full reach forward on rows, controlled lowering. Longer-length/fuller-ROM training is at least as good as short-range work, usually better. **[Grade B]**
- **Row with chest support** when you want to train the mid-back hard without your lower back or momentum becoming the limit. **[Grade C]**
- **Give the rear delts direct work** — a few sets of a cable/machine reverse fly or face-pull; rows alone tend to under-stimulate them. **[Grade C]**
- **Erectors need little direct work** — deadlifts, RDLs, squats, rows, and carries already load them heavily; add back extensions only if they lag. **[Grade C]**
- Lats and upper back tolerate **high volume** (up toward **~10–20+ hard sets/week** near the ceiling); build up to it rather than starting there. **[Grade C]**

## Practical Application

The back rewards treating it as regions with different best tools, not one undifferentiated "pull" day. Picks below are ordered for **hypertrophy** — biasing the loaded stretch, machine/cable stability where it lets you train harder, and lower systemic fatigue — which is exactly how the app's engine ranks them.

**Region 1 — Lats (width).** *Job: shoulder extension and adduction — pulling the upper arm down and toward the body.*
1. [Lat pulldown](../../data/exercises/lat-pulldown.json) — the backbone: full overhead stretch, freely adjustable load, low fatigue cost.
2. [Pull-up](../../data/exercises/pull-up.json) / [chin-up](../../data/exercises/chin-up.json) — same pattern with bodyweight; superb once you can control the full range (add load or assistance to stay in a hypertrophy rep range).
3. [Straight-arm pulldown](../../data/exercises/straight-arm-pulldown.json) — a pure lat isolation that keeps tension through the stretched, overhead portion.
4. [Cable pullover](../../data/exercises/cable-pullover.json) / [dumbbell pullover](../../data/exercises/dumbbell-pullover.json) — deep lat stretch under load.
5. [Single-arm dumbbell row](../../data/exercises/single-arm-dumbbell-row.json) — big range, hits lats and mid-back with minimal lower-back cost.

*Cue:* reach all the way up to lengthen the lat, then drive the **elbow down and in toward the hip** — think of the arm as a hook, not a puller — and don't let it turn into a shrug.

**Region 2 — Mid-back thickness (mid/lower traps, rhomboids).** *Job: scapular retraction — squeezing the shoulder blades together.*
1. [Chest-supported row](../../data/exercises/chest-supported-row.json) — the standout: the pad removes lower-back and momentum limits, so effort caps the set, not your spine.
2. [Seated cable row](../../data/exercises/seated-cable-row.json) — constant tension, easy to load and progress.
3. [T-bar row](../../data/exercises/t-bar-row.json) / [barbell row](../../data/exercises/barbell-row.json) — the most load and the most systemic fatigue; keep them if you like them, but count the cost.

*Cue:* let the shoulder blades spread at the front, then **retract them** and pull to the lower ribs; a brief pause at the squeeze beats heaving a heavier weight.

**Region 3 — Upper traps.** Direct work is optional. Heavy [deadlifts](../../data/exercises/conventional-deadlift.json), carries, and rows already load the upper traps hard; if you specifically want more, add [barbell](../../data/exercises/barbell-shrug.json) or [dumbbell shrugs](../../data/exercises/dumbbell-shrug.json) and hold the top briefly.

**Region 4 — Rear delts.** They're small and rows alone tend to leave them under-stimulated, so give them a few dedicated sets of a [cable rear-delt fly](../../data/exercises/cable-rear-delt-fly.json), [reverse pec-deck](../../data/exercises/reverse-pec-deck.json), or [face-pull](../../data/exercises/face-pull.json). (More in the [Shoulders guide](shoulders.md).)

**Region 5 — Spinal erectors.** Mostly trained **indirectly**: [conventional deadlifts](../../data/exercises/conventional-deadlift.json), [RDLs](../../data/exercises/romanian-deadlift.json), squats, rows, and loaded carries all brace and extend the spine under real load. Add a [back extension](../../data/exercises/back-extension.json), [machine back extension](../../data/exercises/machine-back-extension.json), or [reverse hyper](../../data/exercises/reverse-hyperextension.json) only if the low back lags or you want dedicated work. Because deadlifts are so systemically fatiguing, **count them inside your weekly back volume**, not on top of it.

**Putting a week together.** A simple, complete back week is one vertical pull + one or two horizontal pulls (at least one chest-supported), a couple of rear-delt sets, and whatever erector work your compounds don't already cover — repeated across **two sessions** for most people, since spreading volume over two days beats cramming it into one.

**The universal levers still rule.** These picks simply apply the fundamentals every muscle obeys — enough [volume](../01-training-variables/volume.md), real [effort](../01-training-variables/proximity-to-failure.md) close to failure, and sound [exercise selection](../01-training-variables/exercise-selection-and-order.md), progressed over time — to this region.

## The Evidence

Back growth follows the same levers as every muscle. **Volume within a recoverable range drives hypertrophy** — more hard sets per week means more growth, up to the point you can recover from[^schoenfeld-2017-volume-dose-response] **[Grade A]** — which is why the high-tolerance lats and upper back can productively carry a lot of weekly sets. **Training through a full range, loading the lengthened position**, is favoured by the range-of-motion evidence: fuller-ROM work (the deep overhead stretch on a pulldown, the full reach on a row) matches or beats short-range work for hypertrophy[^schoenfeld-2020-rom-review]. **[Grade B]** Training **multiple pulling angles** — vertical for width, horizontal for thickness — is grounded in the muscles' distinct functions and is standard practice, though head-to-head hypertrophy comparisons of specific pull variations are limited, hence the lower grade. **[Grade C]**

## Key Uncertainties & Nuance
- **You can't reliably sculpt one part of a muscle.** A 2025 meta-analysis on whether training muscle length shifts *regional* growth found that longer- and shorter-length training produced **similar hypertrophy across proximal, mid, and distal sites**, with only trivial differences[^varovic-2025-muscle-length-regional]. **[Grade B]** So "lower-lat" or "upper-lat" exercise-picking is largely a myth — train the lats as a whole through a full range and let the muscle grow along its length. (The general principle: [Regional & architectural hypertrophy](../00-foundations/regional-and-architectural-hypertrophy.md).)
- **The lats are hard to "feel."** A deliberate stretch and a controlled, elbow-led pull matter more than chasing a mind-muscle cue.
- **Rows are a blend.** Any row trains some mix of lats, mid-traps, rhomboids, and rear delts; the exact split shifts with elbow path and grip, so you don't need a separate exercise for each.
- **Deadlifts build the back but cost the most.** They load erectors, traps, and lats heavily while being the most fatiguing thing you can do — budget them into your volume rather than adding them on top.
